07 The Filter: sound in close-up
  • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound

    • 07.01 Modifying sound

      • The MicroFreak has a Low Pass Filter (LPF), a Band Pass Filter (BPF), and a High Pass Filter (HPF). The Type button switches between them.
      • A low-pass filter attenuates frequencies above its cutoff; a high-pass filter attenuates frequencies below it; a band-pass filter attenuates frequencies on both sides of the cutoff.
      • With a low-pass filter open at maximum cutoff, all frequencies pass. Lowering the cutoff removes the highs first, then the midrange; closing it completely leaves silence. In high-pass mode, maximum cutoff removes all frequencies; lowering it lets frequencies above the cutoff pass. In band-pass mode, frequencies near the cutoff remain audible, and resonance sets the width of that range.
      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/01 Low Pass Filter

        • 07.01.01 Low Pass Filter

          • A Low Pass Filter removes frequencies above its cutoff. It is central to subtractive synthesis and selectively reduces a sound's high frequencies. Modulating the cutoff over time varies the sound's timbre.

          • Note/Info The MicroFreak filter has a 12 dB-per-octave roll-off. Its slope is gentler than a 24 dB filter, which suits oscillators with rich, complex harmonics such as grains, wavetables, shapers, and FM.

      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/02 Band Pass Filter

        • 07.01.02 Band Pass Filter

          • A Band Pass Filter lets only a narrow frequency range pass. At minimum resonance, all frequencies pass; turning Resonance clockwise narrows the range. At its maximum, the filter self-oscillates and can act as a sine-wave oscillator, while blocking the Digital Oscillator's sound.
      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/03 High Pass Filter

        • 07.01.03 High Pass Filter

          • A High Pass Filter removes frequencies below its cutoff. Raising the cutoff removes the lows first, then the midrange, leaving the highest harmonics.
          • Use it to accent beats in an arpeggio or bass loop, or to clear low frequencies from a solo sound that competes with a bass part in a mix.
          • Its main controls are Cutoff frequency and Resonance.
      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/04 Cutoff Frequency

        • 07.01.04 Cutoff frequency

          • The cutoff frequency is where filtering takes place. Feeding a filter's output back into itself creates a resonance peak around the cutoff. The Resonance knob controls this effect; the LFO or envelopes can also modulate resonance when assigned in the Matrix.
          • Filters attenuate frequencies gradually above or below the cutoff. This slope, or roll-off, depends on the number of poles: a four-pole filter has a steeper roll-off than a two-pole filter. The MicroFreak filter rolls off at 12 dB per octave.
          • The Cutoff knob moves the cutoff point from about 30 Hz at its fully counter-clockwise position to above 15 kHz at its fully clockwise position.
      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/05 Resonance

        • 07.01.05 Resonance or Q

          • Resonance, also called Emphasis or Q (for quality of filtering), amplifies frequencies close to the cutoff. Turning the Resonance knob clockwise makes the filter more selective and gives the sound a stronger ringing character. At its extreme setting, the filter self-oscillates.
07 The Filter: sound in close-up
  • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound

    • 07.02 Animating sound

      • Changing the cutoff and resonance dynamically makes the filter a musical tool. The MicroFreak can use an LFO, an Arpeggiator, or an Envelope to control these parameters. See Microfreak/UG/08 LFO for the LFO; the Arpeggiator and Envelope chapters cover the other modulators.
      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound/01 Cutoff Modulation

        • 07.02.01 Cutoff Modulation

          • Moving the cutoff changes the sound's timbre. The most effective and musically pleasing way to modulate it is with an envelope generator. The MicroFreak connects the main envelope to filter cutoff; the Filter Amt knob beside Sustain sets the modulation amount.

          • Note/Info Changing Filter Amt lights the corresponding point on the Matrix. The filter amount can also be fine-tuned there.

      • Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound/02 Emphasis Resonance Modulation

        • 07.02.02 Emphasis/Resonance Modulation

          • Increasing emphasis focuses the filter's response around the cutoff, boosting nearby frequencies.

          • Note/Info A sequencer modulation track can create stepped harmonic effects by assigning a different filter-cutoff modulation amount to each step.

08 The LFO
  • Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/01 LFO Shape

    • 08.1 LFO Shape

      • The Shape button selects one of six waveforms: sine, triangle, rising sawtooth, rectangle (square), random (sample and hold), or random gliding (smoothed random).

      • Note/Info The square wave has a 50% duty cycle: it stays high for half of each cycle.

      • The Random wave creates random modulations. For a familiar science-fiction computer sound, select Random, set the LFO speed to about 4 Hz, choose the LFO-to-Pitch crossing in the Matrix, then set the modulation amount to about 30.
      • Random gliding is a slewed random wave. Its pitch changes gradually between random values, unlike the abrupt changes of the Random wave. Routing it to oscillator pitch makes the modulation amount easier to fine-tune before applying it to another target.

      • Note/Info Matrix modulation on the MicroFreak is bipolar: it can affect a target in both positive and negative directions. This offers more sound-design options, though a slow negative modulation of the Analog Filter can make the sound inaudible for part of the cycle.

08 The LFO
  • Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate

    • 08.2 LFO Rate

      • The Rate knob sets the LFO speed from 0.06 Hz to 100 Hz.
      • The LFO does not follow the MicroFreak clock by default. Press the Rate encoder to enable Sync.
      • With Sync off, the display shows the rate in hertz. With Sync on, the LFO follows the MicroFreak clock, a DAW clock, or an external MIDI clock, and the display shows divisions from 8/1 to 1/32: 8/1, 4/1, 2/1, 1/1, 1/2, 1/2t, 1/4, 1/4t, 1/8, 1/8t, 1/16, 1/16t, and 1/32.
      • The displayed ratio sets how the LFO cycle relates to the clock. At 8/1, the slowest rate, a cycle completes in eight pulses. Each step up doubles the LFO speed, except the 1/2t, 1/4t, and 1/8t triplet settings. At 1/4, one LFO cycle equals a quarter note.
      • Matching the LFO rate to the MicroFreak sequencer, a DAW, or another clock source can create rhythms at multiples or divisions of the clock.

      • Note/Info The display shows the LFO sync ratio.

      • Turning Sync off uncouples the LFO from the MicroFreak tempo and restores its 0.06 Hz to 100 Hz range.
      • Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate/01 LFO Retriggering

        • 08.2.1 LFO Retriggering

          • LFO retriggering restarts the LFO each time you press a keyboard key or the arpeggiator or sequencer generates a gate signal. This can make the LFO add a small pitch rise at the start of each note.
          • To enable retriggering, open Utility and set Preset > LFO Retrig to ON.
08 The LFO
  • Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/03 Freaky Tips

    • 08.3 Freaky Tips and Tricks

      • Use the LFO to modulate both the filter cutoff and the envelope attack and decay times. Filter cutoff is a standard Matrix connection; assign the envelope attack and decay destinations explicitly in the Matrix. See Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/02 Matrix and Encoder for details.
      • Adding some randomness to filter cutoff creates a fuzzy effect. Random modulation of an envelope's decay or sustain adds variety to a rhythmic pulse and can revive a stale sound.
      • Use the sine or sawtooth wave to modulate an envelope's level. Playing an arpeggio then produces cyclic crescendos and diminuendos. A very slow LFO can also modulate the Cycling Envelope's rise and fall times; route the resulting envelope to other destinations.
      • Use the rising sawtooth to modulate the decay time of the Standard or Cycling Envelope for more realistic drum or bell sounds. The Cycling Envelope's attack and fall shapes can also change dynamically from linear to exponential. See Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen for details.
      • To animate a sequence or arpeggio, sync the LFO, select Random, and apply a small amount of modulation to oscillator pitch in the Matrix. Modulate the Matrix amount with a very short Cycling Envelope set to ENV, so the LFO modulation ends after the initial attack.
      • Assign Glide amount as an LFO destination to switch Glide on and off by setting its amount to zero. With Sync on, the LFO can activate Glide every other or every fourth sequencer step.

09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/01 What an Envelope Does

    • 09.1 What does an Envelope Generator do?

      • An instrument's envelope and timbre help make it recognizable. An organ reaches full volume at once, stays there while a key is held, then decays quickly. A piano has a slower attack and longer decay. A string section builds and fades more gradually.
      • In electronic music, an envelope can also shape a sound's frequency content. A familiar example is a filter sweep.
      • To hear a filter sweep on the MicroFreak:
        • Set the filter Cutoff to minimum and Resonance to maximum.
        • Set Amp Mod on the Envelope to ON.
        • Set Envelope Attack to 1.7 s, Decay to 7.7 s, Sustain to 90%, and Filter Amount to 70.
      • The filter will sweep through the oscillator's overtones. The effect is stronger with an oscillator rich in harmonics. Try different oscillator models to hear how the filter changes each one.
      • The MicroFreak can use envelopes to shape both a sound's timbre and its amplitude (volume).
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/02 Gates and Triggers

    • 09.2 Gates and Triggers

      • An envelope needs a trigger or a gate to start.
      • A trigger is a very short pulse that can synchronize modules or start the MicroFreak's LFO or envelopes. A gate lasts longer, from a few milliseconds to a few seconds.
      • The keyboard is the MicroFreak's primary gate source. Touching and holding a key starts the envelope's Attack stage, followed by Decay/Release and Sustain. The envelope stays at the Sustain level while the key is held. Releasing the key ends the gate, and the level falls to zero at the rate set by Decay/Release.
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/03 Envelope Stages

    • 09.3 Envelope stages

      • The MicroFreak's Envelope Generator has three stages: Attack, Decay/Release, and Sustain. It is technically an ADS envelope because the keyboard can hold the Sustain stage indefinitely.
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/03 Envelope Stages/01 Attack

        • 09.3.1 Attack

          • Attack sets how long the envelope takes to rise from its minimum to its initial maximum level: from 0 ms to 10 seconds.
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/03 Envelope Stages/02 Decay Release

        • 09.3.2 Decay/Release

          • Decay/Release sets how long the envelope takes to move from its attack level to its Sustain level after Attack ends. Its range is 0 ms to 13 seconds.
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/03 Envelope Stages/03 Sustain

        • 09.3.3 Sustain

          • Sustain sets the level where the signal settles after Decay. This level is usually lower than the initial level, but it can be the same; in that case, Decay does not change the envelope. Use a low Sustain level for percussive sounds.
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/04 Filter Amount

    • 09.4 Filter amount

      • The Standard Envelope can control filter cutoff and amplitude. When Amp Mod is off, the envelope controls filter cutoff. When Amp Mod is on, it controls both volume and filter cutoff.
      • Filter Amount sets how strongly the envelope controls the filter's frequency. It is a bipolar control.
      • Filter Amount is a Matrix destination. Modulating it with the LFO can gradually dampen the sound; the LFO waveform determines how the cutoff changes.
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/05 Amp Mod Button

    • 09.5 The Amp Mod button

      • The keyboard and sequencer gates control the internal Voltage Controlled Amplifier (VCA) directly: a held key turns the sound on, and releasing it turns the sound off.
      • Turning on Amp Mod activates the Envelope Generator to shape the volume with those gates. The envelope output also always goes to the filter; Filter Amount sets its effect on cutoff. The envelope is also available as a Matrix source for controlling other MicroFreak modules.
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope

    • 09.6 The Cycling Envelope Generator

      • Unlike a standard envelope that runs through its stages once, the Cycling Envelope can retrigger itself after its final stage. It can act as a complex LFO, producing wave shapes that a standard LFO cannot.
      • You can change the shapes of the Rise and Fall stages. The Rise, Fall, and Hold levels can be modulated, so the shape can change in real time.
      • The Cycling Envelope Generator
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/01 Cycling Envelope Stages

        • 09.6.1 The stages of the Cycling Envelope

          • The Cycling Envelope has three stages:
            • Rise sets how long the envelope takes to reach its maximum after a keyboard or Arp/Seq trigger.
            • Fall/Shape sets how long the envelope takes to fall to zero.
            • Hold/Sustain, part of the Fall/Shape control, sets the level held during the Hold stage.
          • The Mode button selects Env, Run, or Loop:
            • Env: The envelope runs once and stops at the end of Fall.
            • Run: The envelope free-runs as an LFO and resets when the MicroFreak receives a MIDI Start command.
            • Loop: A keyboard, sequencer, or arpeggiator trigger resets the envelope. It retriggers at the end of Fall in Run and Loop modes.
          • In Env mode, a keyboard or external gate starts Rise. Sustain stays high while the key is held; releasing the key ends the gate and starts Fall. In Run and Loop modes, Hold stays high for the time set with the Hold knob before Fall begins.
          • To hear the stages, connect CycEnv to oscillator Pitch in the Matrix and set the amount to about 20. Set Rise to about 200 ms, Hold to 0, Fall to 0 ms, and Amount to 50%. Pitch rises, then slowly falls; lowering Amount makes the rise less noticeable.
          • In Loop mode, very short Rise, Fall, and Hold settings make the Cycling Envelope cycle quickly as a complex LFO. Change these times to change its wave shape.
          • In Loop mode, trigger the envelope with the keyboard or Arp/Seq. Set the arpeggiator near 55 bpm and hold a chord to hear the envelope retrigger on each step.
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/02 Changing Shapes

        • 09.6.2 About Changing Shapes

          • Hold Shift and turn Rise or Fall to change that stage's shape. Turn left for a logarithmic curve, right for an exponential curve, or use the middle dead zone for a linear curve. The display shows “LINEAR” in that zone.
          • Velocity curves
          • A logarithmic curve rises quickly and slows near its endpoint. An exponential curve rises slowly, then speeds up. A linear curve changes at a steady rate.
          • An exponential Rise with a logarithmic Fall can make the envelope feel sluggish. A logarithmic Rise with an exponential Fall can make it start more sharply and end more reluctantly.
      • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/06 Cycling Envelope/03 Legato Options

        • 09.6.3 Using the Legato options

          • Legato connects each new note to the previous one without a gap. Staccato playing lifts the fingers before playing the next note.
          • A Utility setting controls how the Envelope and Cycling Envelope respond to legato in monophonic mode. When the setting is OFF, each added note restarts both envelopes. When it is ON, only the first key starts them; later keys use the contour already in progress.
09 The Envelope Generator
  • Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/07 Cycling Envelope Suggestions

    • 09.7 Freaky Cycling Envelope Suggestions

      • Use the Matrix to modulate the Cycling Envelope's Rise, Hold, and Fall with the LFO or pressure. Pressure gives direct control over the Rise and Fall stages.
      • Matrix modulation can be positive or negative. A negative signal to Fall shortens the Fall stage as pressure increases.
      • Amount controls how strongly the Cycling Envelope affects its destinations. Adjust it carefully, especially when modulating the analog filter. A control that reduces signal strength is an attenuator.
      • The Matrix can combine the Cycling Envelope with stages of the Main Envelope. For example, modulating Attack changes its slope; modulating Decay changes its length. Further Matrix routings can build longer modulation chains.
      • Patch ideas:
        • Use a slow sine LFO to control Cycling Envelope Rise, then use CycEnv to control Main Envelope Sustain.
        • Use a random LFO to control Cycling Envelope Amount, then use CycEnv to control Main Envelope Decay/Release or Sustain.

10 The Keyboard Section
  • Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/01 Gates and Triggers

    • 10.1 Another Look at Gates and Triggers

      • The envelope section introduced gates and triggers. These signals also matter when playing the keyboard, which is the MicroFreak's primary source of gates.
      • Touching and holding a key produces a gate. The gate ends when the finger lifts. When the Envelope's Amp | Mod button is off, the internal amplifier listens for a key press and turns on abruptly. This is an on/off response, but other settings can change how it feels.
10 The Keyboard Section
  • Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/02 Keyboard Responsiveness

    • 10.2 Keyboard Responsiveness

      • Set the keyboard to send aftertouch or velocity at Utility → Preset → Press Mode. This choice changes how the keyboard responds to touch.
      • Utility → Preset → Velo Amp Mod sets how strongly velocity affects the patch volume, from 0 to 10.

      • Note/Info Assign Press as the Matrix source and the Envelope's Sustain knob as a modulation target to experiment with keyboard and volume effects.

      • Hearing is more sensitive to pitch changes than to volume changes, so pitch can help when adjusting keyboard responsiveness. On an empty preset, assign pressure to pitch in the Matrix at the maximum amount. Hold one finger at a 90° angle against the upper edge of a key, then lower it so more skin touches the key. The increased contact sends more pressure and raises the pitch.
      • Pressing hard while keeping the finger at 90° will not reach maximum pressure because of the touch-plate design.

      • Note/Info Velocity, Aftertouch, and Velo Amp Mod settings are saved with each preset. Two presets with the same sound can use different Velo Amp Mod values, such as 5 and 10, for different keyboard volume response.

    • 10.3 Glide

      • The Glide knob controls how quickly pitch moves between notes. It smooths the abrupt pitch change from one key to another, with a time from off to about 10 seconds.
      • The Glide Knob
      • Glide appears in musical styles around the world. Indian vocal phrases and sitar string bends use it; in Western music, this kind of phrasing is called melisma.

      • Note/Info Utility settings can make the Glide knob use a fixed time, a beat-synced value, or a rate tied to the Arp/Seq rate.

      • In Time mode, glide ranges from zero to 10 seconds. Synced mode offers 1/32T, 1/32, 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/8, 1/4T, 1/4, 1/2T, 1/2, and 1/1. In Rate mode, the time to glide within an octave ranges from immediate at 0 ms to more gradual at 10 ms.
      • Pressure can modulate Glide: set Glide as a Matrix destination and Pressure as the source, then adjust the Matrix attenuator. Different LFO waveforms produce different glide intensity and slope. With LFO Sync on, each note gets a different glide shape; changing the LFO rate changes the synchronized rate and the glide speed.
      • Tip: The Sequencer's modulation tracks can also control Glide:
        • 1. Turn on the Sequencer with Shift + Arp | Seq.
        • 2. Select sequencer A or B.
        • 3. Press Record and step-record a melody. Recording stops automatically at the end of the sequence.
        • 4. Press Record again, then move the Glide knob where you want a glide effect in the sequence.

    • 10.4 Octave Buttons

      • The Octave buttons transpose the keyboard's output up or down by octaves.
      • The Octave Buttons
      • The buttons shift the pitch by up to three octaves in either direction. They blink faster as the shift moves farther from zero, reaching their fastest rate at -3 and +3 octaves. The two-octave keyboard can therefore cover an eight-octave pitch range.

      • Note/Info The octave shift is saved with the preset.
