10.7 Editing MIDI Clips
  • Operations on a whole MIDI clip beyond those in Ableton/Live/UG/08 Clip View.
10.7 Editing MIDI Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/07 Editing MIDI Clips/01 Cropping MIDI Clips

    • 10.7.1 Cropping MIDI Clips

      • Crop Clip deletes MIDI outside the loop brace; Crop to Time Selection deletes outside a time selection. Right-click the clip or press Cmd+Shift+J (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+J (Win). No new file is written.
10.7 Editing MIDI Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/07 Editing MIDI Clips/02 The Time Commands in the MIDI Note Editor

    • 10.7.2 The …Time Commands in the MIDI Note Editor

      • Cut, Copy and Paste act on notes; the "… Time" commands insert or remove time in the whole clip, without moving its start, end or loop brace.
        • Duplicate Time copies the selected span and its notes into the clip.
        • Delete Time removes it and closes the gap.
        • Insert Time adds empty time the length of the selection, before it.
10.7 Editing MIDI Clips
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/07 Editing MIDI Clips/03 Looping

    • 10.7.3 Looping

      • Move the loop start and end markers, even during playback, to loop the passage you're editing.
      • With the loop brace selected, Cmd+D (Mac) / Ctrl+D (Win) doubles the loop with its notes, moving later notes along and zooming out to fit.
      • MIDI clips loop by default; the Clip Loop toggle turns it off and grays out the brace.

10.8 Multi-Clip Editing
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing/01 Focus Mode

    • 10.8.1 Focus Mode

      • Focus Mode edits one clip while the others stay visible. Toggle it with the Focus button or N; hold N while editing to switch it temporarily.
      • On: the active clip's notes and loop bar are in color, the rest gray, with its name below the loop bars. Hover another clip's loop bar to preview it, click to switch. Edits and the displayed scale apply to the active clip; Fold to Notes considers all clips.
      • Off: every note is active and colored. Clicking a loop bar turns it black so Velocity or Chance randomization targets that clip. Root and scale show only when shared, otherwise an asterisk. Edits apply to all selected clips; notes can be cut, copied and pasted across clips, and time selected across clip and loop boundaries.
10.8 Multi-Clip Editing
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing/02 Multi-Clip Editing in the Session View

    • 10.8.2 Multi-Clip Editing in the Session View

      • Up to eight looped clips; loop bars ordered by track, then scene.
      • Clips of different lengths repeat until they realign; time outside a clip's loop is shaded darker. Hovering shows its loop start and end as black lines, shifted if its start marker comes before the loop.
10.8 Multi-Clip Editing
  • Ableton/Live/UG/10 Editing MIDI/08 Multi-Clip Editing/03 Multi-Clip Editing in the Arrangement View

    • 10.8.3 Multi-Clip Editing in the Arrangement View

      • Clips from up to eight tracks within a time selection; loop bars ordered by track vertically and by time horizontally.
      • Notes can be drawn across clip boundaries, except in Focus Mode. The editor spans from the first clip's start to the last clip's end. A mix of looped and unlooped clips shows the Loop button half colored.

11. MIDI Tools
  • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/01 Using MIDI Tools

    • 11.1 Using MIDI Tools

      • MIDI Tools live in the Clip View's Transform and Generate panels. Transformations need selected notes and replace them; MPE tools are transformations that add or change note expression, visible in the MPE Editor. Generators need no input and create notes.
      • Native tools are built in and fixed; Max for Live tools can be edited, built, or installed from other makers, and show under the browser's MIDI Tools filter group or the MIDI Tool tag.
      • Pick a tool from the panel's selector and adjust it. Auto Apply, on by default, applies changes immediately; turn it off, which restores the original notes, to adjust first and then press Apply. Cmd+Enter (Mac) / Ctrl+Enter (Win) applies from the editor.
      • Transformations act on the time selection, the note selection, or the whole loop. Generators fill the time selection or loop, keeping existing notes they don't overlap and replacing those they do.
      • With a clip scale, pitch parameters use scale degrees.
      • Undo and Redo affect notes, not tool settings; Reset returns a tool's parameters to default without touching notes.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/01 Using MIDI Tools/01 Using Max for Live MIDI Tools

        • 11.1.1 Using Max for Live MIDI Tools

          • Max for Live MIDI Tools are .amxd files (Ableton/File/.amxd) edited in a Max patcher like any Ableton/Max for Live device. Velocity Shaper and Euclidean come with Live Standard and Suite; building or editing tools needs Suite or the Max for Live add-on.
          • Third-party tools appear when saved in ~/Music/Ableton/User Library/MIDI Tools/Max Transformations or .../Max Generators, or in any folder in Places.
11. MIDI Tools
  • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools

    • 11.2 Transformation Tools

      • The transformations included with Live.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/01 Arpeggiate

        • 11.2.1 Arpeggiate

          • Turns the selected notes into an arpeggio, using the Arpeggiator effect's engine and its 18 Style patterns.
          • Distance transposes successive steps by semitones or scale degrees; Steps sets how many transposed steps. Rate sets the pattern speed and note length; Gate shortens notes below 100% and lengthens them above.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/02 Chop

        • 11.2.2 Chop

          • Divides notes into 2 to 64 Parts, and builds division patterns of up to 16 parts that repeat.
          • Gaps adds rests: a positive value puts a gap after that many notes, a negative value puts that many gaps after each note. Pattern toggles show the result and let you add or remove gaps by hand, which sets Gaps to a dash.
          • Emphasis toggles pick notes or gaps to lengthen by the Stretch Chunk(s) factor, 2 to 8 times. Variation randomizes note starts and ends.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/03 Connect

        • 11.2.3 Connect

          • Fills the gaps between notes with new notes at random positions.
          • Spread limits how far their pitch can stray from the originals; Density sets what share of each gap is filled, all of it at 100%. Rate sets their length; Tie is the chance a note extends to the next original note.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/04 Glissando

        • 11.2.4 Glissando

          • An MPE tool that bends from one note's pitch to the next, tying them; needs at least two notes.
          • Start sets where the bend begins as a share of the note; Curve shapes it. Drag the yellow breakpoint or the line in the display.
          • The bend shows only in the MPE Editor, or in the Pitch Bend expression lane when notes are folded.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/05 LFO

        • 11.2.5 LFO

          • An MPE tool that writes a low-frequency oscillator into pitch bend, slide or pressure, chosen with Target.
          • Envelope Attack and Decay shape the amplitude across the oscillation and trade off against each other. Shape Type is Sine, Square, Triangle or Random (with Reseed), and Shape adjusts it.
          • Rate runs from 1 (four beats) to 1/128; Time Shift delays the start or shifts phase.
          • Amount sets depth: up to twice the clip's pitch bend range for pitch, for example ±120 semitones for a ±60 range, or -127 to 127 for slide and pressure. Amplitude Base sets the centre value within the same kinds of limits.
          • Results show in the MPE Editor and its expression lanes, not in the Note Editor.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/06 Ornament

        • 11.2.6 Ornament

          • Adds ornamental notes before the selected notes; applying again adds more.
          • Flam adds one note. Flam Position, as a share of a grid step, places it over the note's start (positive) or before it (negative); Flam Velocity sets its velocity relative to the original.
          • Grace Notes adds several equal notes. Pitch: High or Low alternates a semitone or scale degree above or below, Same keeps the pitch. Position, Velocity and Chance work like Flam's, relative to the original note; Amount sets how many.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/07 Quantize

        • 11.2.7 Quantize

          • Moves or stretches selected notes to the grid or a chosen value, including triplets, quantizing starts, ends, or both. Amount moves notes only part of the way. Audio clips have a matching tool.
          • Edit → Quantize Settings… opens it; Cmd+Shift+U (Mac) / Ctrl+Shift+U (Win) quantizes without opening the panel.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/08 Recombine

        • 11.2.8 Recombine

          • Swaps one property among the selected notes, chosen with Dimension: Position, Pitch, Duration or Velocity.
          • Shuffle permutes at random, a new result each Apply; Mirror reverses the order; Rotate shifts values around by Rotation Steps, set in the display or with the arrows, up to one less than the note count. Positive rotates clockwise.
          • For Position, Rotate on Grid counts grid cells instead of notes.
          • The three combine in the order Shuffle, Mirror, Rotate.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/09 Span

        • 11.2.9 Span

          • Sets note lengths by articulation:
            • Legato: each note reaches the next; the last reaches the end of the selection or loop.
            • Tenuto: lengths stay as they are.
            • Staccato: every note becomes half the shortest gap between note starts.
          • Offset lengthens or shortens note ends by up to a grid step. Variation shortens (positive) or lengthens (negative) notes at random, anew each time.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/10 Strum

        • 11.2.10 Strum

          • Staggers the start times of a chord's notes, like strumming.
          • Strum Low offsets from the lowest note and Strum High from the highest, each up to a grid step forward (positive) or back (negative); at least one must be non-zero. Set them in the Strum Position display or by typing.
          • Tension curves the spacing: positive values start wide and narrow; negative values start tight and widen. At 0 the spacing is even.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/11 Time Warp

        • 11.2.11 Time Warp

          • Stretches selected notes along a speed curve, for accelerando or ritardando. The curve spans the time selection and has one to three breakpoints, dragged in the display or set with Breakpoint Time and Speed.
          • Quantize snaps results to the grid; Preserve Time Range keeps them within the original span; Include Note End lets the curve change note lengths too.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/02 Transformation Tools/12 Velocity Shaper

        • 11.2.12 Velocity Shaper

          • A Max for Live tool that shapes the selected notes' velocities with an envelope; click to add breakpoints and drag them.
          • Minimum and Maximum Velocity set the range. Loop repeats the shape across the selection; Rotate shifts it by steps of Division, for example one grid step.
11. MIDI Tools
  • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools

    • 11.3 Generative Tools

      • The generators included with Live.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/01 Rhythm

        • 11.3.1 Rhythm

          • Generates a repeating rhythm on one pitch or drum pad, chosen with Pitch or by Option-clicking (Mac) / Alt-clicking (Win) the piano ruler.
          • Steps (up to 16), Pattern (the arrangement of hits; choices depend on Steps and Density), and Density (how many notes).
          • Step Duration sets the step length and so how often the pattern repeats in the selection: in one bar, 8 steps of 1/8 play once, of 1/16 twice. Split is the chance a step divides in two; Shift moves the pattern by steps.
          • Velocity and Accent set normal and accented velocity; Accent Frequency sets how many notes apart accents fall (1 accents every note), and Accent Offset moves them.
          • Deselect the result and run it again on another pitch or pad to layer rhythms.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/02 Seed

        • 11.3.2 Seed

          • Generates random notes within ranges. Set the pitch range with its sliders or handles, or Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Win) on the piano ruler; overlapping the handles gives a single pitch. The slider is purple when a scale is active.
          • Duration (1/128 to a whole note) and Velocity (1 to 127) ranges work the same way.
          • Voices caps simultaneous notes; Density sets how many notes, as a share of the pitch range.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/03 Shape

        • 11.3.3 Shape

          • Generates a run of notes that follows a shape, chosen from Shape Presets or drawn in the display, within a pitch range set by sliders or by Option-dragging (Mac) / Alt-dragging (Win) the piano ruler. Purple means a scale is active.
          • Rate sets the shortest note; Tie is the chance a note extends to the next; Density sets how much of the shape is filled.
          • Jitter randomizes pitch away from the shape while staying within the range.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/04 Stacks

        • 11.3.4 Stacks

          • Adds chords or progressions within the scale, filling the time selection or loop.
          • Choose a chord by dragging on the Chord Selector Pad or with Cmd (Mac) / Ctrl (Win) and up or down; diagrams follow the Tonnetz, and the Status Bar names the chord.
          • Custom chord banks are JSON .stacks files. Put one in a folder in Places and double-click it to load; the Stacks tag finds them. Saved banks live in the User Library's Chord Banks folder.
          • Add Chord (+) and Delete Chord (−) build a progression; the display shows the selected chord's settings.
          • Chord Root sets the root, limited to the clip scale's notes and defaulting to its root; Option-click (Mac) / Alt-click (Win) the piano ruler or use the arrow keys. Chord Inversion cycles inversions, negative values an octave lower. Chord Duration and Offset adjust length and position in eighths of the chord.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/11 MIDI Tools/03 Generative Tools/05 Euclidean

        • 11.3.5 Euclidean

          • A Max for Live generator for Euclidean rhythms on up to four voices, in the time selection or loop.
          • Pattern tab: a picture of the rhythm, on/off toggles and a Rotation slider per voice, and a button that randomizes rotations.
          • Voices tab: on/off toggles, a pitch or drum pad per voice (arrows shift them all), and per-voice Velocity.
          • Steps sets the pattern length, repeating and wrapping across the selection; Density sets how often it repeats; Division sets the step length.

      • Steps sets the pattern length, repeating and wrapping across the selection; Density sets how often it repeats; Division sets the step length.

12. Editing MPE
  • Controller setup is covered in Ableton/Live/UG/34 MIDI and Key Remote.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/01 Viewing MPE Data

    • 12.1 Viewing MPE Data

      • Open a clip in Clip View and choose the Note Expression tab (Option+3 on Mac, Alt+3 on Windows).
      • Slide, Pressure, Velocity and Release Velocity each get an expression lane; only Slide and Pressure show by default. Pitch envelopes draw on top of their notes in the MIDI Note Editor.
      • Toggle buttons on the left show or hide each lane; the triangle button below them shows or hides all enabled lanes at once. Option-click (Mac) or Alt-click (Windows) the triangle to show every lane.
      • Lanes resize individually by their split lines, or together by dragging the line between the lanes and the note editor.
      • MIDI track meters light their lowest dot blue when per-note controller changes pass through.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/02 Editing MPE Data

    • 12.2 Editing MPE Data

      • Click a note while the Note Expression tab is open to bring its Pitch, Slide and Pressure envelopes forward; Velocity and Release Velocity appear as markers. Other notes are greyed out.

      • Breakpoints and segments

        • Click a line segment to add a breakpoint; click a breakpoint to delete it. Values show while creating, hovering or dragging.
        • Right-click a breakpoint → Edit Value to type an exact value; right-click a preview breakpoint → Add Value to create one at an exact value.
        • Drag a breakpoint or segment to move it. Selected breakpoints move together.
        • Option-drag (Mac) / Alt-drag (Windows) a segment to curve it; double-click with the same modifier to straighten it.
        • Hold Shift while dragging to lock to one axis, or to fine-tune vertically.
        • Dragging a breakpoint horizontally over its neighbour removes the neighbour.

      • Grid

        • The grid is off by default in this tab and its settings are saved with the clip, separate from the other tabs. Turn it on with Snap to Grid or Cmd+4 (Mac) / Ctrl+4 (Windows).

      • Whole-note edits

        • Slide, Pressure, Velocity and Release Velocity envelopes can be scaled across the whole note: click outside the note, hover until the envelope turns blue, then drag up or down. Pitch can't be scaled this way.
        • Cmd+A / Ctrl+A on an envelope selects all its breakpoints so they can be shifted by the same amount.
        • Moving or stretching a note moves or stretches its expression with it.
        • Pitch breakpoints snap to the nearest semitone while holding Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) with the grid off.
        • Pitch envelopes hide when Fold is on; Z zooms to fit the pitch bend in the time selection; Clear All Envelopes in the context menu removes a selected note's expression.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/03 Drawing Envelopes

    • 12.3 Drawing Envelopes

      • With Draw Mode on, drag freehand in the Pitch, Slide and Pressure lanes to draw an envelope.
      • Toggle Draw Mode with B, the Control Bar's Draw Mode switch, or the Options menu. Holding B turns it on only while held.
      • Shift while dragging gives finer values. With the grid on, drawing makes steps as wide as the visible grid; hold Cmd (Mac) / Alt (Windows) to draw on the grid while it is off.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/04 MPE in Live's Devices and on Push 2

    • 12.4 MPE in Live’s Devices and on Push 2

      • Many of Live's own instruments support MPE and ship with MPE presets. They also respond to polyphonic aftertouch from Push 2; see Ableton/Live/UG/36 Push 2.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/05 MPE in External Plug-ins

    • 12.5 MPE in External Plug-ins

      • Live can read and modulate MPE data for plug-ins that support it.
      • A plug-in's MPE Mode on/off state is saved with its default configuration. Plug-ins with MIDI outputs can send MPE as well.
12. Editing MPE
  • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings

    • 12.6 MPE/Multi-channel Settings

      • The MPE/Multi-channel Settings dialog sets up MPE output for hardware synths with particular zone needs, or for multi-timbral plug-ins that can be played from an MPE controller without official MPE support.
      • It opens from the Ext. Instrument device, from a MIDI track's I/O section in the mixer, or from the context menu of an MPE-enabled plug-in.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings/01 Accessing the MPE-Multi-channel Settings Dialog

        • 12.6.1 Accessing the MPE/Multi-channel Settings Dialog

          • Ext. Instrument: pick a target in MIDI To, choose MPE in the second menu, then open that menu again and choose MPE Settings….
          • Mixer I/O section: with the target device chosen in the track's MIDI To, choose MPE in the second menu, then reopen it and choose MPE Settings…, in either the Session or Arrangement mixer.
          • Plug-ins: right-click the device's title bar.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/12 Editing MPE/06 MPE-Multi-channel Settings/02 The MPE-Multi-Channel Settings Dialog

        • 12.6.2 The MPE/Multi-Channel Settings Dialog

          • The dialog chooses the MPE zone (lower or upper) and how many note channels it uses, or switches to multi-channel mode with any range of note channels.
          • Each zone has a global channel for controls that aren't per-note: channel 1 for the lower zone, channel 16 for the upper. The number of note channels sets the zone's polyphony, for example 1–11 for the lower zone and 12–16 for the upper.
          • A track outputs to one zone only, so using both zones takes two tracks. Zones apply only to MPE output.
          • Two tracks routed to the same MIDI output, one per zone, can drive two MPE synths chained through MIDI thru, or two sounds in one synth.

13. Converting Audio to MIDI
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track

    • 13.1 Slice to New MIDI Track

      • Slicing cuts the audio into pieces of time, one per MIDI note, without analysing pitch or rhythm. The pieces keep playing the original sound.
      • The dialog slices by beat division, by transients or by Warp Markers, and offers a Slicing Preset. A Rack holds at most 128 chains, so Live refuses settings that would make more than 128 slices.
        • Your own slicing presets go in the Defaults folder of the User Library.
        • Preserve warped timing keeps timing changes from warping; with it off, slices sound like the raw audio.
      • The result:
        • a new MIDI track with a clip holding one note per slice, in a rising chromatic sequence;
        • a Ableton/Instrument/Drum Rack on that track with one chain per slice, each a Ableton/Instrument/Simpler loaded with its slice;
        • the Drum Rack's Ableton/Macro controls mapped to envelope, loop and crossfade settings across every Simpler, as set by the slicing preset.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/01 Resequencing Slices

        • 13.1.1 Resequencing Slices

          • The sliced clip forms a rising "staircase" of notes. Editing the notes, or dragging Drum Rack pads onto each other to swap their notes, plays the slices in a new order.
      • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/01 Slice to New MIDI Track/02 Using Effects on Slices

        • 13.1.2 Using Effects on Slices

          • Each slice has its own Drum Rack chain, so it can take its own audio effects. To share effects across several slices, select their chains and press Cmd+G (Mac) / Ctrl+G (Windows) to group them into a nested Rack, then add effects after it.
          • MIDI effects placed before the Drum Rack, such as Arpeggiator or Random, rearrange the slices as they play.
          • Slicing suits drum loops but also works on voices and ambient textures.
13. Converting Audio to MIDI
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/02 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

    • 13.2 Convert Harmony to New MIDI Track

      • Detects the pitches in polyphonic audio and writes them to a clip on a new MIDI track, which comes with an Instrument Rack playing a piano sound.
      • Unlike slicing, the Convert commands don't play the original audio; the notes drive a different instrument.
      • Works on recordings from your collection, or on your own playing of guitar, piano or other harmonic instruments.
13. Converting Audio to MIDI
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/03 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

    • 13.3 Convert Melody to New MIDI Track

      • Detects the pitches in monophonic audio and writes them to a new MIDI track with a synth Instrument Rack. Its "Synth to Piano" Macro moves the sound from an analog-style synth to an electric piano.
      • Singing, whistling or a solo instrument can all become MIDI notes this way.
13. Converting Audio to MIDI
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/04 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

    • 13.4 Convert Drums to New MIDI Track

      • Extracts rhythms from unpitched, percussive audio onto a new MIDI track with a Drum Rack, trying to sort hits into kick, snare and hi-hat.
      • Adjusting the audio clip's transient markers before converting changes where notes land.
      • Suits breakbeats, beatboxing or tapping on a surface.
13. Converting Audio to MIDI
  • Ableton/Live/UG/13 Audio to MIDI/05 Optimizing for Better Conversion Quality

    • 13.5 Optimizing for Better Conversion Quality

      • For the most accurate conversions:
        • use material with clear attacks — notes that swell in may be missed;
        • use isolated instruments, since every instrument present gets detected;
        • use uncompressed audio (.wav, .aiff) or high-bitrate lossy files.
      • Live places note boundaries at the clip's transient markers, so adding, moving or deleting markers before converting tunes the result.
      • The "wrong" command can be useful: Convert Harmony on a monophonic vocal tends to add notes that weren't there.
