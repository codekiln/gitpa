Music/Scale/Blues

Page reference

  • The blues came out of African American vocal music in the Deep South around 1900. Its "blue notes" are inflected regions around the third, fifth and seventh that a singer or guitarist bends into, and they fall between equal-tempered pitches.
Music/Scale/Dorian

Page reference

  • In plainchant and Renaissance polyphony, the D mode was defined by its final, its range and its reciting tone A. B♭ was common, so much Dorian music sounds Aeolian to modern ears.
  • Baroque composers often wrote D minor with no key signature, a habit inherited from Dorian notation. Person/Johann Sebastian Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor BWV 538 is called "Dorian" for that reason alone: its notation has no flat, and its music is in D minor.
  • The i–IV vamp, a minor tonic with a major subdominant, is Dorian's signature in rock and Latin music, as in Person/Tito Puente's "Oye Como Va" and the rock band Santana's cover of it, with Person/Carlos Santana on guitar.

Music/Scale/Hirajoshi

Page reference

  • Hirajōshi is a tuning for the thirteen strings of the koto, credited to Person/Yatsuhashi Kengyō in the seventeenth century. It became the standard tuning for solo koto music of the Edo period, including his Rokudan no shirabe.
  • In Western rock, metal and film music, the scale stands in for Japan.

Music/Scale/Pentatonic/Major

Page reference

  • Five-note scales without semitones appear in traditions with no historical contact: Chinese, Korean, Japanese, West African, Andean, Scottish and Appalachian music among them. Chinese theory derived its five tones, gong shang jue zhi yu, from a chain of fifths by the third century BCE.
  • European art music took it up as a marker of the exotic or the rural. Person/Claude Debussy heard the Javanese gamelan at the 18/8/9 Exposition Universelle in Paris, and "Pagodes" (Estampes, 1903) is built on pentatonic collections. Javanese slendro tuning divides the octave into five near-equal steps, so the Western pentatonic is only an approximation of what he heard.
  • Music education uses it for improvisation because it contains no semitones and no tritone. The Orff Schulwerk and Kodály methods start children on it.
Music/Scale/Pentatonic/Minor

Page reference

  • Person/Béla Bartók and Kodály's fieldwork after 1905 found that the oldest layer of Hungarian peasant song is in a minor pentatonic, often with a melody that repeats a fifth lower in its second half. Bartók set out that finding in The Hungarian Folk Song (1924). It overturned the nineteenth-century assumption that Hungarian music meant the style hongrois of Music/Scale/Minor/Hungarian.
