Microfreak/UG/07 Filter
  • A filter emphasizes or suppresses harmonics in a sound, changing its timbre. It is traditionally paired with an oscillator; the MicroFreak's analog filter shapes the sound of its Digital Oscillator.
Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound
  • The MicroFreak has a Low Pass Filter (LPF), a Band Pass Filter (BPF), and a High Pass Filter (HPF). The Type button switches between them.
Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/01 Low Pass Filter

  • Note/Info The MicroFreak filter has a 12 dB-per-octave roll-off. Its slope is gentler than a 24 dB filter, which suits oscillators with rich, complex harmonics such as grains, wavetables, shapers, and FM.

Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/01 Modifying Sound/04 Cutoff Frequency
  • Filters attenuate frequencies gradually above or below the cutoff. This slope, or roll-off, depends on the number of poles: a four-pole filter has a steeper roll-off than a two-pole filter. The MicroFreak filter rolls off at 12 dB per octave.
Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound
  • Changing the cutoff and resonance dynamically makes the filter a musical tool. The MicroFreak can use an LFO, an Arpeggiator, or an Envelope to control these parameters. See Microfreak/UG/08 LFO for the LFO; the Arpeggiator and Envelope chapters cover the other modulators.
Microfreak/UG/07 Filter/02 Animating Sound/01 Cutoff Modulation
  • Moving the cutoff changes the sound's timbre. The most effective and musically pleasing way to modulate it is with an envelope generator. The MicroFreak connects the main envelope to filter cutoff; the Filter Amt knob beside Sustain sets the modulation amount.

Microfreak/UG/08 LFO
  • An LFO (short for low-frequency oscillator) produces waveforms at sub-audio frequencies. These waveforms can modulate other parts of the MicroFreak, such as:
    • The oscillator pitch
    • The filter cutoff frequency
    • The filter emphasis
    • The stages of an envelope
  • The MicroFreak LFO
Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/01 LFO Shape

  • Note/Info Matrix modulation on the MicroFreak is bipolar: it can affect a target in both positive and negative directions. This offers more sound-design options, though a slow negative modulation of the Analog Filter can make the sound inaudible for part of the cycle.

Microfreak/UG/08 LFO/02 LFO Rate
  • The LFO does not follow the MicroFreak clock by default. Press the Rate encoder to enable Sync.
  • With Sync off, the display shows the rate in hertz. With Sync on, the LFO follows the MicroFreak clock, a DAW clock, or an external MIDI clock, and the display shows divisions from 8/1 to 1/32: 8/1, 4/1, 2/1, 1/1, 1/2, 1/2t, 1/4, 1/4t, 1/8, 1/8t, 1/16, 1/16t, and 1/32.
  • Matching the LFO rate to the MicroFreak sequencer, a DAW, or another clock source can create rhythms at multiples or divisions of the clock.
  • Turning Sync off uncouples the LFO from the MicroFreak tempo and restores its 0.06 Hz to 100 Hz range.

Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen
  • The Envelope Generator is one of the MicroFreak's basic building blocks. It shapes a sound's overall loudness or timbre and can send modulation to any Matrix destination, including destinations you create.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/01 What an Envelope Does
  • To hear a filter sweep on the MicroFreak:
    • Set the filter Cutoff to minimum and Resonance to maximum.
    • Set Amp Mod on the Envelope to ON.
    • Set Envelope Attack to 1.7 s, Decay to 7.7 s, Sustain to 90%, and Filter Amount to 70.
  • The MicroFreak can use envelopes to shape both a sound's timbre and its amplitude (volume).
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/02 Gates and Triggers
  • A trigger is a very short pulse that can synchronize modules or start the MicroFreak's LFO or envelopes. A gate lasts longer, from a few milliseconds to a few seconds.
  • The keyboard is the MicroFreak's primary gate source. Touching and holding a key starts the envelope's Attack stage, followed by Decay/Release and Sustain. The envelope stays at the Sustain level while the key is held. Releasing the key ends the gate, and the level falls to zero at the rate set by Decay/Release.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/03 Envelope Stages
  • The MicroFreak's Envelope Generator has three stages: Attack, Decay/Release, and Sustain. It is technically an ADS envelope because the keyboard can hold the Sustain stage indefinitely.
Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen/05 Amp Mod Button
  • Turning on Amp Mod activates the Envelope Generator to shape the volume with those gates. The envelope output also always goes to the filter; Filter Amount sets its effect on cutoff. The envelope is also available as a Matrix source for controlling other MicroFreak modules.

Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard
  • One of the first capacitive keyboards appeared on the EMS Synthi AKS. In 1972, Don Buchla introduced the Buchla Easel, whose touch-sensitive keys did not move and could produce accurate pressure and voltage-controlled portamento. The capacitive keyboard became a hallmark of the Easel, though few people could afford one. Decades later, the Arturia MicroFreak brought the capacitive keyboard back.
  • The MicroFreak has 25 capacitive keys. Hundreds of copper-colored dots on the surface register touch as either aftertouch or velocity, according to the Utility setting at Preset → Press.
  • A capacitive keyboard makes the player part of its electrical circuit. Changing how much skin touches a key changes its resistance and the pressure signal it sends. Finger contact alone can reach about 30% of the pressure range; pressing more of the finger onto the key can reach 100%. This touch response helps make the MicroFreak a performance instrument.

  • Note/Info Very dry skin can make the keyboard respond poorly. Moisturizer or staying hydrated may help. Never put liquid on the MicroFreak.

  • The keyboard sends gate, pitch, and pressure signals. The Matrix can route these signals to any destination in its top row, and the outputs are also available on the back of the MicroFreak for modular gear or another synthesizer with voltage inputs.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/01 Gates and Triggers
  • The envelope section introduced gates and triggers. These signals also matter when playing the keyboard, which is the MicroFreak's primary source of gates.
Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/02 Keyboard Responsiveness/01 Using Responsiveness
  • Keyboard Pressure or Velocity can control modular gear. Set the keyboard's output voltage at Utility → CV/Gate → Pressure Range, from 0V to 10V. The signal is available at the MicroFreak's rear Pressure output and can open an external filter, change a sequencer's speed, or alter a resonator's damping.
