02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/01 Usage Precautions

    • 02.01 Usage Precautions

      • Use only the power adapter supplied by Arturia. Arturia is not responsible for damage caused by an unauthorized power supply.
      • The touch-capacitive keyboard can work with a power bank, but the MicroFreak must be grounded to function fully. Arturia recommends the supplied three-pin wall plug.
      • After electrostatic discharge interference, the headphone output may go silent and the keyboard may malfunction. Restart the instrument to recover.
      • Prolonged radio-frequency interference may disrupt the keyboard. Restart the instrument to recover.
02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/02 Warning

    • 02.02 Warning

      • Keep power cords and connecting cables out of walkways, where they could be stepped on or rolled over.
      • Arturia does not recommend extension cords. If one is necessary, it must handle this product's maximum current; consult a local electrician about power requirements.
      • Use only components supplied or recommended by Arturia, and follow the safety markings and instructions supplied with those components.
02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/03 Notice

    • 02.03 Notice

      • The manufacturer's warranty does not cover service charges caused by not knowing how a correctly functioning feature works. Read this manual and consult your dealer before requesting service.
02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/04 Precautions

    • 02.04 Precautions include, but are not limited to, the following:

      • Read and understand all instructions. Follow the instructions printed on the instrument.
      • Before cleaning, disconnect the power plug and USB cable. Use a soft, dry cloth. Avoid gasoline, alcohol, acetone, turpentine, other organic solvents, liquid cleaners, sprays, and overly wet cloths.
      • Keep the instrument away from water and moisture. Place it somewhere stable where it cannot fall.
      • Do not put heavy objects on the instrument or block its ventilation openings. Keep it away from heat vents and places with poor air circulation.
      • Use only the supplied AC adapter, and check that local line voltage matches the adapter's specified input voltage.
      • Do not open the instrument or insert objects into it; this can cause fire or electric shock. Do not spill liquid on it.
      • If the instrument malfunctions, take it to a qualified service center. Opening and removing the cover voids the warranty; improper testing may cause electric shock or other malfunctions.
      • Do not use the instrument during thunderstorms, in hot sunlight, or near a gas leak.
      • Arturia is not responsible for damage or data loss caused by improper operation.
      • Arturia recommends shielded audio cables shorter than 3 meters and ferrite-equipped CV/Gate cables.
02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/05 Register Instrument

    • 02.05 Register your Instrument

      • Registering the MicroFreak establishes legal ownership and provides access to Arturia Technical Support and update notices. Registration also offers an option to receive Arturia news and promotions.
      • Sign in to your Arturia account, open My Registered Products, and add the MicroFreak using the serial number on the sticker underneath the instrument.
02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/06 Connecting to the World

    • 02.06 Connecting the MicroFreak to the World

      • Turn off all audio equipment before connecting anything to avoid damage to speakers, the MicroFreak, or other equipment.
      • After making connections, set all levels to zero. Power on the devices with the audio amplifier or monitoring system last, then raise the volume to a comfortable level.

      • Connectors

        • Audio output: 6.35 mm (1/4-inch) TS or balanced TRS output
        • Headphones: 3.5 mm (1/8-inch) TRS jack; mono signal
        • MIDI input and output: 1/8-inch TRS jacks
        • USB: standard USB Type-B
        • Power: DC input, 2.1 mm inner diameter and 5.5 mm outer diameter

      • Note/Info Use the included gray 1/8-inch TRS to 5-pin DIN adapter to connect external MIDI devices.

02 Installation
  • Microfreak/UG/02 Installation/07 Latest Firmware

    • 02.07 Get the Latest Firmware

      • The latest MicroFreak firmware adds features described in Microfreak/UG/01 Welcome and Introduction/02 New in FW 5.0.0. Install the latest firmware to get the full MicroFreak experience.

      • Download the firmware

        • Open the Arturia Downloads and Manuals page.
        • Find MIDI Control Center in the sidebar and install the latest macOS or Windows version.
        • Find MicroFreak in the same sidebar and download its latest firmware.

      • Install the firmware

        • Connect the MicroFreak over USB and power it on, then open MIDI Control Center.
        • Select MicroFreak from the DEVICE menu in the upper-left corner. The menu displays the current firmware revision:
        • Click Firmware Revision to open the dialog showing the current version:
        • Click Upgrade, then use the operating system file dialog to select the downloaded firmware file.

      • Note/Warning While the firmware update is in progress, do not turn the MicroFreak's knobs, power it off, or disconnect its USB cable.

03 MicroFreak Overview
  • This chapter introduces the controls on the MicroFreak. If synthesis is new to you, Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc, Microfreak/UG/07 Filter, and Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen explain its main building blocks.
03 MicroFreak Overview
  • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel

    • 03.01 Front panel overview

      • The MicroFreak's front panel is compact.
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row

        • 03.01.01 Top Row

          • Top Row
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/01 Matrix

            • 03.01.01.01 The Matrix

              • The matrix
              • The Modulation Matrix is an electronic patchbay that routes modulation sources to destinations. Turning the white Matrix encoder moves the connection LED between patch points; after the last point, it cycles back to the first. Click the encoder to set the selected connection's modulation amount.
              • Each connection acts like a patch cord with an attenuator for positive or negative modulation. Parameters with knobs can generally be modulation destinations.
              • Sources occupy rows 1–5. Destinations occupy columns 1–7: columns 1–4 are fixed, while columns 5–7 are assignable.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/02 Paraphonic

            • 03.01.01.02 Paraphonic

              • When the Paraphonic button is lit, the MicroFreak can trigger four voices independently. The voices share one analog filter but can have different amplitudes. Internal VCA envelopes shape each voice's volume along with the Main Envelope.
              • The Main Envelope, Pressure, Keyboard, and Arpeggiator can produce polyphonic modulation. Routing them to polyphonic destinations such as Oscillator Type, Wave, Timbre, or Shape gives each voice its own modulation.
              • Selecting paraphonic mode
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/03 Panel Select

            • 03.01.01.03 Panel Select

              • A loaded preset recalls saved parameter values, which may differ from the panel's current knob positions. Press Panel to apply the current knob positions to the sound, so subsequent edits match what you hear.
              • Panel Select
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/04 Display Preset

            • 03.01.01.04 The Display and the Preset encoder

              • The OLED display shows information about knobs and buttons. Turn the Preset encoder beside it to browse presets; the display shows each preset's name and category.
              • Firmware 5.0.0 increased capacity from 384 to 512 slots. A bank of 64 new presets can be loaded from the MIDI Control Center. Empty presets default to the name “Init” and category “Bass.”

              • Note/Info Factory presets are protected against overwrite by default. Change this in Utility > Misc > Mem Protect.

              • The Preset manager
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/05 Save

            • 03.01.01.05 Save

              • Press Save to save a preset. Microfreak/UG/04 Presets explains the steps.

              • Note/Info Saving is unavailable in Utility mode. Leave Utility mode before saving a preset.

              • Saving Presets
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/06 Utility

            • 03.01.01.06 Utility

              • Utility contains global settings and settings for each preset, including Preset Volume, Bend Range, and Pressure mode.
              • Access to the Utility settings
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/01 Top Row/07 Master Volume

            • 03.01.01.07 Master Volume

              • Master Volume sets the global output level for both the line and headphone outputs. To balance one preset against another, set Utility > Preset > Preset volume.
              • The Volume knob
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row

        • 03.01.02 Middle Row

          • Middle row of the MicroFreak
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/01 Keyboard Glide

            • 03.01.02.01 Keyboard glide

              • Glide smooths the pitch transition between notes. Fully counterclockwise, the transition is immediate; turning the knob clockwise increases the glide time. Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard/03 Glide explains Glide in detail.
              • The Glide knob
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/02 Digital Osc

            • 03.01.02.02 Digital oscillator

              • The Digital Oscillator generates the MicroFreak's core sound. The Analog Filter, Envelopes, and LFO shape it. Type, Wave, Timbre, and Shape control its parameters; Microfreak/UG/06 Dig Osc explains them in detail.
              • The Digital Oscillator
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/03 Analog Filter

            • 03.01.02.03 Analog filter

              • The Analog Filter emphasizes or suppresses harmonics in the Digital Oscillator sound, changing its timbre. Cutoff sets where filtering begins; Resonance emphasizes frequencies near that point. Microfreak/UG/07 Filter explains the filter in detail.
              • The Analog Filter
              • The three filter types
                • Low-pass (LPF) attenuates frequencies above cutoff.
                • Band-pass (BPF) attenuates frequencies above and below cutoff.
                • High-pass (HPF) attenuates frequencies below cutoff.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/02 Middle Row/04 Cycling Env

            • 03.01.02.04 Cycling Envelope

              • The Cycling Envelope generates modulation signals for destinations in the Matrix. Unlike a standard envelope, it can retrigger after its last stage finishes. Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen covers its controls.
              • The Cycling Envelope Generator
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row

        • 03.01.03 Bottom Row

          • Bottom row of the MicroFreak
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/01 Octave Select

            • 03.01.03.01 Octave select

              • The octave buttons select the active keyboard range.
              • Octave select
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/02 Shift

            • 03.01.03.02 Shift

              • Hold Shift to access functions printed in blue on the panel and other secondary functions. Microfreak/UG/20 Appendix C - Cheat Sheet lists them.
              • The shift button
              • Shift functions
                • Toggle between Arpeggiator and Sequencer.
                • Set the swing rate.
                • Control the Attack and Fall shapes of the Cycling Envelope.
                • Transpose or reload sequences, or copy an arpeggio to a sequence.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/03 Arp Seq

            • 03.01.03.03 ARP/Seq (Arpeggiator/Sequencer)

              • The Arpeggiator plays notes derived from the held keys. Microfreak/UG/12 Arpeggiator and Microfreak/UG/13 Sequencer share several controls.
              • The Arpeggiator and the Sequencers
              • Arp | Seq switches between the Arpeggiator and Sequencer. Oct | Mod sets the Arpeggiator range or selects one of four Sequencer modulation tracks.
              • Rate sets the tempo in BPM. Press the knob to enable Sync; with Sync active, the knob changes the time division relative to the set tempo.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/04 LFO

            • 03.01.03.04 LFO

              • The low-frequency oscillator produces modulation at 0.05–100 Hz and offers six waveforms.
              • The LFO
              • Shape selects a waveform
                • Sine rises and falls smoothly.
                • Triangle rises and falls linearly.
                • Rising sawtooth rises linearly and then drops suddenly.
                • Square switches suddenly between minimum and maximum.
                • Random stepped jumps between randomly generated values.
                • Random gliding moves gradually between randomly generated values.
              • Rate sets LFO speed. Press it to enable Sync, which follows the Sequencer/Arpeggiator tempo clock. With Sync off, Rate sets the speed independently.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/05 Gen Env

            • 03.01.03.05 The General Envelope Generator

              • The Envelope Generator shapes a tone’s loudness or timbre and can be patched to destinations in the Matrix. Attack, Decay/Release, and Sustain affect the filter by default; Filter Amt sets the envelope’s effect on the filter. Microfreak/UG/09 Envelope Gen explains the controls in detail.
              • The Envelope Generator
              • With Amp Mod active, the envelope also shapes VCA loudness.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/06 Keyboard

            • 03.01.03.06 Keyboard section

              • The 25-key capacitive keyboard generates gate, pitch, and pressure. The octave buttons extend its two-octave range. The section also includes the Icon strip and the Bend, Spice, and Dice touch controls.

              • Note/Info Utility can make the keyboard generate velocity in place of pressure.

              • The Keyboard
              • Utility or MIDI Control Center settings determine whether the keyboard provides aftertouch or velocity. USB and MIDI out can send fully polyphonic MIDI control to other devices.
          • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/01 Front Panel/03 Bottom Row/07 Icon Strip

            • 03.01.03.07 The Icon strip

              • The eight icons above the keyboard provide Arpeggiator, Pattern Generator, Sequencer, Spice, Dice, and Bend controls.
              • The icon strip
              • Spice and Dice vary the current arpeggio or sequence; their effects are audible only while the Arpeggiator or Sequencer runs. Dice changes gates and triggers, while Spice sets the amount of variation.
              • Spice & Dice
              • Microfreak/UG/10 Keyboard explains the Icon strip in detail.
03 MicroFreak Overview
  • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel

    • 03.02 Rear Panel Overview

      • Rear Panel Overview
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/01 Audio Outputs

        • 03.02.01 Audio outputs

          • The headphone output is a 3.5 mm TS or TRS jack. The MicroFreak sends the same mono signal to both sides of stereo headphones.
          • Audio output
          • The headphone jack also accepts the microphone supplied with the MicroFreak Vocoder Edition. A TRRS device, such as a gaming headset with a built-in microphone, can serve as an audio input on a standard MicroFreak.

          • Note/Warning Pull the microphone straight out of the headphone jack to avoid damage. Remove it before storing or transporting the MicroFreak.

          • The Line output is a balanced 6.35 mm TRS jack for an amplifier or mixer. A TRS cable improves the signal-to-noise ratio. Its signal is mono.
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/02 Pitch Gate Pressure

        • 03.02.02 Pitch/Gate/Pressure outputs

          • These outputs send electrical signals to external analog synthesizers or a Eurorack modular system.
          • CV, Gate and Pressure outputs
          • CV sends a control voltage for an external oscillator; Gate triggers an external device.
          • Pressure sends a pressure or velocity voltage, selected in Utility > Preset > Press mode.
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/03 Clock

        • 03.02.03 Clock input/output

          • Sync the MicroFreak with external synthesizers or modular systems through the Clock input and output.
          • Clock input and output

          • Note/Info A TRS jack carries both clock and start signals. A TS jack carries only clock.

      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/04 MIDI

        • 03.02.04 MIDI input/output

          • MIDI input and output
          • Use the included gray 3.5 mm TRS-to-5-pin-DIN adapters to send and receive MIDI and controller data from compatible devices.
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/05 USB DC In

        • 03.02.05 USB/DC IN

          • The USB connector supplies power and exchanges data with a computer. A 5 V, 500 mA USB phone charger can also power the MicroFreak without a computer.
          • The USB connector
          • USB connects the MicroFreak to Arturia MIDI Control Center for settings, firmware updates, and preset management.
      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/06 Power Switch

        • 03.02.06 Power switch

          • The recessed switch turns the MicroFreak off without unplugging USB. When both USB and DC power are connected, DC power takes priority; connecting DC power resets the MicroFreak.
          • The On-Off switch

          • Note/Info The MicroFreak can run from a phone or tablet power bank.

      • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/02 Rear Panel/07 Power Connector

        • 03.02.07 Power Connector

          • Connect the supplied Arturia power supply to the mains outlet. The supply provides the ground required by the capacitive keyboard.
          • The power connector
03 MicroFreak Overview
  • Microfreak/UG/03 Overview/03 Signal Flow

    • 03.03 Signal Flow

      • The Digital Oscillator sends its waveform to the Filter and an analog VCA. The main Envelope has a fixed connection to the VCA. With Amp Mod on, the main Envelope controls the VCA; with Amp Mod off, the keyboard Gate controls it.
      • The signal flow of the MicroFreak
      • In Paraphonic mode with Amp Mod on, copies of the envelope control internal digital VCAs, one for each voice set in Utility.
      • The main Envelope also controls Filter cutoff through the Filter Amount knob. Turning the knob lights the envelope-to-filter connection in the Matrix.

04 The MicroFreak Presets
  • Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/01 Load Presets

    • 04.01 Loading Presets

      • Turn the Preset encoder to load a preset. The first 128 slots contain factory presets. If you want to keep those intact while learning synthesis, use slots 129–512 for your sounds.
      • Presets in slots 129–512 are templates for building sounds in specific categories. A preset you like can also serve as a template for further exploration.
      • Tweak the loaded preset, then save it if you want to keep your changes.

      • Note/Warning By default, loading another preset clears unsaved changes without warning. Turn on Utility > Global > Controls > Click to Load to browse without replacing the current sound; click the encoder to save the current changes and load the selected preset.

      • Press the Preset encoder quickly three times to reset the current sound to its initial empty state. This also clears an existing preset's sound.

      • Note/Info The reset does not change the stored preset unless you save afterward.

      • The Preset encoder
04 The MicroFreak Presets
  • Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/02 Save Presets

    • 04.02 Saving Presets

      • Press Save to enter save mode. From there you can save to the current slot or another location, change the category, or rename the preset.

      • Note/Info Press Save again to cancel; the display shows “Save Cancelled.” To save changes in the current slot, hold Save until the display shows “Preset saved.”

      • To use another slot, turn the encoder to select a location and press it. When the display shows “Click to save,” choose one of the eleven categories: bass, brass, keys, lead, organ, pad, percussion, sequence, sfx, strings, or template.
      • Press the encoder again to name the preset. The Save button blinks while you enter the name. Turn the encoder to cycle through uppercase letters, lowercase letters, then the digits 0–9; turn it all the way left to enter a space.
      • Push and turn the Preset encoder to edit a character in an existing name. Hold Shift while turning the encoder to scroll through characters faster.
      • Click to accept each character and move to the next field. When the name is complete, press Save to store the preset.
      • Saving a preset stores the knob positions, sequences, modulation tracks, and Utility settings specific to that preset.
      • To arrange presets for a song, place them in consecutive slots in MIDI Control Center, then load the reordered presets into the MicroFreak.
      • The Template category holds presets you want to explore further. Use one as a starting point, then save variations in other slots.
      • On startup, the MicroFreak loads the most recently saved preset.

      • Note/Info Chord settings and the last-used chord are saved with a preset. Spice & Dice settings are not.

      • Note/Warning If the display says “Memory protect is on,” set Utility > Misc > Mem Protect to Off.

04 The MicroFreak Presets
  • Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/03 Preset Config

    • 04.03 Tweaking the Preset Configurations

      • Utility contains settings that change how the current preset behaves. Each preset saves its own settings, so presets can differ in voice mode, pressure response, sequence length, and other behavior.

      • Note/Info The two sequences in a preset always have the same length.

      • Changing Utility > Preset settings can reshape a sequence. For example, change Glide Mode from Time to Rate, turn on Envelope Reset for a snappier sequence, or change Seq (1–4) Smooth to hear how smoothing affects its mood.
      • The following preset parameters can be edited in Utility. None can be edited in MIDI Control Center.

      • Voice

        • Bend Range [0–24]: Sets the range from zero to two octaves.
        • Glide Mode [Time, Sync, Rate]: Selects time-based, tempo-synced, or rate-based glide for the Glide knob.
        • Unison Spread [0.001–12.000]: Sets the spread between the four voices.
        • Unison Count [2–4]: Sets how many voices take part in Unison mode.

      • Modulations

        • Envelope Legato [Off, On]: Resets the envelope on a keyboard trigger.
        • Envelope Snap [Off, On]: Creates a sharper envelope decay.
        • LFO Retrig [Off, On, Legato]: Sets whether the LFO retriggers, retriggers on a keyboard trigger, or does not retrigger during legato playing.
        • Press Mode [Aftertouch, Velocity]: Selects the pressure mode.
        • Key/Arp Mode [Linear, Random]: In Random mode, generates a new random value for each key pressed.
        • Velo Amp Mod [0–10]: Sets how velocity affects the output volume.

      • Preset Volume

        • Preset Volume [−12 to +12]: Sets the preset's relative volume.

      • Scale

        • Scale: Selects Global, Off, Major, Minor, Harmominor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Blues, or Penta.
        • Root Note: Selects C, C♯, D, D♯, E, F, F♯, G, G♯, A, A♯, or B.

      • Seq/Arp

        • Seq Length [4–64]: Sets the sequence length.
        • Default Gate Length [5–85]: Sets the default sequencer or arpeggio gate length; the default is 45.
        • Seq 1 Smooth [Off, On]: Smooths the sequencer MOD track 1.
        • Seq 2 Smooth [Off, On]: Smooths the sequencer MOD track 2.
        • Seq 3 Smooth [Off, On]: Smooths the sequencer MOD track 3.
        • Seq 4 Smooth [Off, On]: Smooths the sequencer MOD track 4.

      • Vocoder

        • Vocoder Hiss Mode [Off, Switched, Pass]: Sets whether hiss is heard while using the vocoder.
        • Vocoder Hiss Volume [−20 to 0]: Sets the vocoder hiss volume.

      • After loading a preset, the MicroFreak plays the sound stored in memory. The panel's current knob positions may differ from those saved positions, so moving a knob may not immediately match the sound you hear.

04 The MicroFreak Presets
  • Microfreak/UG/04 Presets/05 Digital Control

    • 04.05 Understanding Digitally-Controlled Analog

      • Every MicroFreak module, including the analog filter, is controlled digitally. This combines the warmth and direct control of analog sound with the ability to save and recall presets and settings.
      • Knobs and sliders send instructions to the digital circuitry; they do not control voltages directly. After loading a preset, the physical control positions may not match the stored settings.
      • MIDI Control Center offers three ways to match a control's position to its stored value:
        • Hook: Move the control until it reaches the stored position; it has no effect before then.
        • Jump: The value jumps to the control's position as soon as it moves.
        • Scaled: The control's range scales with the stored value and the distance from its physical position to either end.
      • In the default mode, move the knob past its stored value to hook it. Press Panel to bypass the loaded preset and hear a sound based on the panel's current knob and slider positions.

      • Note/Info To arrange presets for a song, save them in consecutive MIDI Control Center slots and load the reordered presets into the MicroFreak.

05 Making Connections
  • Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/01 Control Signals

    • 05.1 Control Signals

      • The Matrix connects control signals from the MicroFreak's modules. These signals move more slowly than audio and are suited to controlling sound.
      • The MicroFreak Matrix
      • Control signals usually move between 0 and 100 Hz. They can modulate the Digital Oscillator, Analog Filter, and other destinations. The Matrix encoder sets their amount from -100% to +100%.
      • The MicroFreak's modules generate control signals in different ways.
        • The LFO makes slow, regular waves. Routed to oscillator pitch, it makes the pitch rise and fall; it can reach 100 Hz.
        • An envelope rises once, then gradually fades. Routed to oscillator pitch, it creates a quick rise followed by a descent. The Cycling Envelope can repeat, acting as a second LFO with more complex modulation waves.
      • A gate rises at Note On and falls at Note Off. The keyboard sends gates that start the MicroFreak's envelope.
      • Three kinds of control signals are used: triggers, gates, and waves.
        • Triggers are very short spikes that start an envelope, LFO, or sequencer. Clocks generate triggers.
        • Gates last longer and keep something active, such as an envelope's hold stage. A keyboard sends a gate while a key is held.
        • Waves can last any length and usually cycle from high to low and back. The MicroFreak's LFO and two envelopes create slow waves.
      • Control signals give a MicroFreak performer room to develop a personal style, as color and line do for a painter.
      • Note for advanced users: Analog synthesizers and modular systems use control voltages for modulation. Mostly digital synthesizers such as the MicroFreak use digital signals that mimic those voltages; this manual calls them control signals.
05 Making Connections
  • Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/02 Matrix and Encoder

    • 05.2 The Matrix and its encoder

      • The Matrix links control signals from sources to destinations.
      • A classic synthesizer sends an oscillator through a filter and then an amplifier. The Matrix lets you override those fixed connections and route triggers, gates, LFO waves, and envelopes to the Digital Oscillator and Filter.
      • The Matrix is the main switchboard for creating, changing, and mixing modulation connections. It has a switchboard and an encoder for selecting connections and setting their modulation amount.
      • The Matrix and its encoder
      • Turn the encoder to choose a source–destination point, then press it to create or edit the connection. It scrolls forward or backward and wraps at the end of the Matrix.
      • The Matrix shows each connection's state:
        • LED off: no connection is active, or its amount is zero.
        • LED on: a connection is active.
        • LED blinking: the connection is selected and its modulation amount is being edited.
      • The display shows the selected connection and its current modulation amount. Press the encoder to edit the amount; the display inverts and only that connection's LED stays lit. Changes take effect immediately.
      • Multiple sources can control the same destination. For example, Key/Arp and the LFO can both affect oscillator pitch, and their modulation amounts are added together. With a rectangular LFO wave, this can transpose a sequence up and down at the LFO rate.
      • Setting a connection's amount to zero turns it off. To clear a Matrix point and reset its amount, hold the encoder for at least 0.5 seconds.
      • To create and edit a connection:
        • Turn the encoder to select a point and press it.
        • Turn the encoder to set a positive or negative modulation amount.
      • To disable a connection and reset its amount:
        • Select the connection with the encoder.
        • Hold the encoder for at least 0.5 seconds, or set its amount to zero.
      • Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/02 Matrix and Encoder/01 Sources and Destinations

        • 05.2.1 Sources and Destinations

          • The Matrix has 35 patch points: five sources on the left and seven destinations across the top.
          • Its sources are:
            • CycEnv: output of the Cycling Envelope Generator.
            • ENV: output of the Envelope Generator.
            • LFO: output of the Low-Frequency Oscillator.
            • PRESSURE: pressure or velocity output, depending on the Utility setting.
            • KEY / ARP: combined output of the keyboard, arpeggiator, and sequencer.
          • The first four destinations are Pitch, Wave, Timbre, and Cutoff.
            • Pitch, Wave, and Timbre modify the Digital Oscillator's sound.
            • Cutoff changes the Filter's cutoff frequency. The Digital Oscillator passes through the Analog Filter, which can remove or emphasize frequencies. See Microfreak/UG/07 Filter.
      • Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/02 Matrix and Encoder/02 Assigning Destinations

        • 05.2.2 Assigning destinations

          • Assign1, Assign2, and Assign3 are user-defined destinations. They can turn nearly any MicroFreak knob into a modulation destination.
          • Master Volume and the Preset Encoder cannot be assigned. Shift-plus-knob parameters, control buttons, and icon buttons such as Spice and Dice cannot be assigned either.
          • To assign a destination to one source:
            • Select its Assign point in the Matrix and press the encoder to enter edit mode.
            • Hold the corresponding Assign button and turn the knob to use as a destination. The display confirms the parameter.
            • Turn the encoder to set the modulation amount, then press it to leave edit mode.
          • To assign one destination to every source in a column, hold an Assign button and move the desired knob. Then set the amount for each source in that column.
          • Possible modulation destinations include:
            • Glide: Glide amount.
            • Oscillator Type: oscillator model.
            • Sample: sample slot index.
            • Oscillator Wave, Timbre, and Shape: the corresponding parameter of the selected oscillator.
            • Filter Cutoff and Resonance: cutoff frequency and filter bandwidth.
            • Envelope Attack, Decay, Sustain, and Filter Amount: the Standard Envelope stages and its amount sent to the amplifier.
            • LFO Rate: LFO speed.
            • Arp&Seq Rate: arpeggiator and sequencer rate.
            • Cycling Envelope Rise, Fall, and Hold: the Cycling Envelope's stages.
            • Cycling Envelope Amount: amount sent from CycEnv to the Matrix.
            • Matrix Modulation Amount: modulation amount of a Matrix point.
          • Starting with MicroFreak firmware 5.0.0, Sample can be assigned by opening the Sample select menu, pressing one of the three Assign buttons, and turning the Type knob.
          • Sample assigned as a Matrix destination
          • A Matrix connection's modulation amount can itself be modulated. To vary vibrato depth with the Cycling Envelope:
            • Route the LFO to oscillator Pitch.
            • Select CycEnv→Assign1 in the Matrix and hold Assign1.
            • Select the LFO→Pitch point and press the encoder to make it the modulation-amount destination.
            • Return to CycEnv→Assign1 and set its amount. The Cycling Envelope now changes the vibrato depth.
          • In Paraphonic mode, all voices are assigned simultaneously as a destination.
          • To clear all Matrix routings, hold Shift and press the Matrix encoder.
          • The Matrix routes sources to destinations and mixes multiple sources at one destination. The encoder sets modulation strength in either a positive or negative direction.
05 Making Connections
  • Microfreak/UG/05 Connections/03 Freaky Ideas

    • 05.3 Freaky ideas

      • Modulation often works best below full strength; the right amount can add subtlety and expression to a patch. The Matrix offers many ways to find unusual sounds.
      • Change pitch scope: The keyboard and a sequence already modulate oscillator pitch at one volt per octave. Routing Key/Arp to Pitch can widen sequence steps to two or four semitones; negative modulation can shrink them to microtonal intervals.
      • Sum modulation signals: Combine the Standard and Cycling Envelope outputs at one destination to create a complex control signal for the filter or arpeggiator rate.
      • Mix the Cycling Envelope and LFO: Combining them at Filter Cutoff creates a composite modulation that can behave in surprising ways.
      • Modulate the Digital Oscillator: Let an unsynced LFO and Cycling Envelope control Wave, Timbre, and Shape at different speeds to create evolving timbral patterns.
      • Create regular automatic transpositions: Use the LFO's Random wave to modulate Pitch, or a slow square wave for more regular transpositions. The square-wave approach also works with a sequence.
      • Modulate modulation: Sequencer modulation tracks can change the strength of a Matrix connection. Hold an Assign button, select a Matrix point, then release the button to set that point as a destination. The LFO and Cycling Envelope can also modulate connection strength.
      • Modulate Cycling Envelope timing: Use the LFO to control its own rate, or use the Cycling Envelope to control its own rise time, fall time, or amount.
      • Create circular routings: Let the LFO control the Cycling Envelope's rise or fall time, then let the Cycling Envelope control its own level. The EMS Synthi became known for these distinctive, difficult-to-repeat routings.
