17.2 External Audio In/Out
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/02 External Audio In-Out/01 Mono-Stereo Conversions

    • 17.2.1 Mono/Stereo Conversions

      • A mono input records mono samples; anything else records stereo. Inside the device chain the signal is always stereo.
      • Mono becomes stereo by copying the signal to both sides. Routing to a mono output sums left and right and lowers the result 6 dB to avoid clipping.
      • See also Ableton/Track/Q/How can I turn a mono signal into stereo in a channel?

17.3 External MIDI In/Out
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/03 External MIDI In-Out/01 MIDI Port Inputs and Outputs

    • 17.3.1 MIDI Port Inputs and Outputs

      • Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI lists every MIDI input and output port. Each can be used by any number of tracks.

      • 17.3.1.1 Track

        • Sends or receives notes and CC messages.
        • Turn it on for an input to play or record notes, or to record CC from a hardware synth into clips.
        • Turn it on for an output to play external hardware, send notes to another app through a virtual MIDI bus, or send CC automation out.
        • A controller's output port needs Track only if the controller makes its own sound.

      • 17.3.1.2 Sync

        • Sends or receives MIDI Clock, or receives MIDI Timecode. A controller needs it only if it has a sequencer or arpeggiator.
        • Input: to follow an external sequencer, drum machine, groovebox or another DAW. Output: to drive those from Live, or to sync an external synth's LFO and arpeggiator.
        • Expanding an output port with Sync on shows more options:
          • MIDI Clock Sync Delay — delays outgoing sync in milliseconds to fix timing offsets.
          • MIDI Clock Type — Song sends Song Position Pointer and Continue whenever the play position changes; Pattern sends only Start, at the next bar, for devices that ignore SPP.
          • Hardware Resync — what the Control Bar's Resync External Hardware button sends to bring synced gear that has fallen out of time back in line: Stop and Start (default), Start Only, or Don't Resync.

      • 17.3.1.3 Remote

        • Lets a controller map to Live's parameters, and sends feedback back to it.
        • Input: for custom MIDI mappings, or to launch clips from a keyboard. Launchpad/Custom/Mode/1 Arrangement needs Remote on for the Launchpad's input.
        • Output: for controllers whose LEDs or motorised faders show the state of mapped parameters.
17.3 External MIDI In/Out
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/03 External MIDI In-Out/02 Playing MIDI With the Computer Keyboard

    • 17.3.2 Playing MIDI With the Computer Keyboard

      • Turn on the computer MIDI keyboard with the Control Bar button, Options → Computer MIDI Keyboard, or M.
      • The middle letter row plays the white keys starting at C3; the row above plays the black keys.
      • Z and X shift the octave; C and V change velocity in steps of 20.
      • Between C3 and C4 the middle row lines up with Impulse's sample slots, for playing drum patterns.
      • While on, it takes over keys used for other shortcuts. Add Shift to reach single-letter shortcuts, such as Shift+S for solo.
17.3 External MIDI In/Out
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/03 External MIDI In-Out/03 Connecting External Synthesizers

    • 17.3.3 Connecting External Synthesizers

      • To play an external synth, set the track's Output Type to the synth's MIDI port and Output Channel to its channel.
      • The External Instrument device does the same from inside the device chain, and also brings the synth's audio back into the same track. See Ableton/How To/Record MIDI and Audio from external device.
      • When one keyboard synth is both your controller and a sound source, turn on its Local Off so Live becomes the hub that passes keyboard and clip MIDI to it.
17.3 External MIDI In/Out
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/03 External MIDI In-Out/04 MIDI In-Out Indicators

    • 17.3.4 MIDI In/Out Indicators

      • The Control Bar has three pairs of MIDI lights. In each pair the top flashes on incoming MIDI and the bottom on outgoing.
      • From left to right: MIDI Clock and Timecode (shown only when a port has sync enabled); remote control of Live's interface; MIDI to and from MIDI tracks.
      • MIDI used by a remote mapping doesn't reach the tracks. The lights show when that is why a track hears nothing.

17.5 Internal Routings
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/05 Internal Routings/01 Internal Routing Points

    • 17.5.1 Internal Routing Points

      • When a track's Audio From is another track, the Input Channel chooser picks where the signal is tapped:
        • Pre FX — straight from the track, before its devices and mixer.
        • Post FX — after the devices, before the mixer.
        • Post Mixer — the track's final output after volume and pan.
      • Soloing the tapping track lets you hear the tapped track at Pre FX and Post FX, but not at Post Mixer.

      • 17.5.1.1 Routing Points in Racks

        • Every chain of an Instrument or Effect Rack, and every return chain of a Drum Rack, offers the same three tap points, listed as Rack | Chain | Pre FX, Post FX or Post Mixer. Post Mixer is the chain's output just before the chains are summed.
        • Soloing a track that taps a chain still lets you hear that point.
17.5 Internal Routings
  • Ableton/Live/UG/17 Routing and IO/05 Internal Routings/02 Making Use of Internal Routing

    • 17.5.2 Making Use of Internal Routing

      • 17.5.2.1 Post-Effects Recording

        • To record a guitar already processed by a gate or amp model, put the effects on a "Guitar" track with Monitor set to In, and record on other tracks whose input is the Guitar track at Post FX (or Post Mixer to include its level and pan). Set those tracks' Monitor to Off.

      • 17.5.2.2 Recording MIDI as Audio

        • When a complex instrument turns one note into something closer to a passage, record its audio: one MIDI track hosts the instrument, and an audio track taking input from it records the result.

      • 17.5.2.3 Creating Submixes

        • Route several drum tracks to one submix audio track that goes to Main, so one fader controls the whole kit.
        • A Group Track sets up the same routing automatically and can fold its tracks away.
        • Or set each track's Output Type to Sends Only and use a send, so a return track acts as the submix.

      • 17.5.2.4 Several MIDI Tracks Playing the Same Instrument

        • Point a second MIDI track's Output Type at the instrument track, and choose the instrument itself (not Track In) as Output Channel. Both tracks then play one instrument, so sound changes affect both.
        • To mute each part separately, move the original clips to a third track and leave the instrument alone on its track, with Input Type set to No Input so it has no Arm button.

      • 17.5.2.5 Tapping Individual Outs From an Instrument

        • Impulse offers each of its eight sample slots as a source for other tracks. An audio track whose input is the Impulse track can choose one slot, which takes that slot out of Impulse's own mix for separate effects. Most plug-ins don't remove the tapped output this way.

      • 17.5.2.6 Using Multi-Timbral Plug-In Instruments

        • A multi-timbral instrument plays a different part on each MIDI channel. Extra MIDI tracks feed its parts by pointing Output Type at the instrument's track and Output Channel at each part's channel; audio tracks tap its individual outputs.
        • External Instrument devices on other MIDI tracks can do both at once: set MIDI To to the instrument's track and channel, and Audio From to one of its auxiliary outputs. Several can share one track inside a Rack. The instrument's main outputs stay on its own track.

      • 17.5.2.7 Feeding Sidechain Inputs

        • A vocoder on a strings track needs a speech signal at its sidechain input: create a "Speech" track whose Output Type is the strings track and whose Output Channel is the vocoder's sidechain. Vocoder plug-ins with their own carrier synth go on a MIDI track and are fed the same way.
        • Many of Live's built-in devices have their own sidechain choosers, so they don't need this.

      • 17.5.2.8 Layering Instruments

        • To double a strings part with brass, add a MIDI track with a brass instrument whose Input Type taps the strings track at Post FX. MIDI from another track is tapped after its MIDI effects and just before its instrument, so the brass gets the same notes.
