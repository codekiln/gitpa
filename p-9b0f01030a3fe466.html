  • 19.3 Recording

    • Record in Arrangement View to capture several tracks at once and watch the take build along the timeline. Record in Session View to split a take into clips on the fly or to record while launching clips.

      • 19.3.1 Recording Into the Arrangement

        • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Button/Arrangement Record starts recording. With Start Playback with Record on (Record, Warp & Launch Settings) it starts at once; with it off, recording waits for Play or a clip launch. Shift-click does the opposite of the setting.
        • Every armed track gets a new clip.
        • With MIDI Arrangement Overdub on, new MIDI clips combine what was already in the track with the new input. Audio tracks don't overdub.
        • Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Punch-In blocks recording before the Arrangement Loop's start, which protects earlier material and gives a pre-roll. Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Switch/Punch-Out stops it after the loop's end.
        • Recording into the Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Toggle/Arrangement Loop keeps every pass. Undo repeatedly to step back through them, or open the clip: the sample holds all passes, the loop brace marks the last, and moving the markers left plays earlier ones.
        • See also Ableton/Arrangement View/Q/Can I record in Arrangement View while playing Session View clips?

      • 19.3.2 Recording Into Session Slots

        • Set Global Ableton/Live/UI/Control Bar/Menu/Quantization to anything but None so clips are cut on time.
        • Arm the tracks; empty slots on armed tracks show Clip Record buttons.
        • Session Record records into the selected scene on every armed track; the new clips' launch buttons turn red. Press it again to switch straight to looped playback.
        • Or click one Clip Record button to record a single slot, and press the clip's Launch button to loop it.
        • A slot's Clip Stop button, or the Control Bar's Stop, stops the clip.
        • The New button stops clips on armed tracks and selects a scene ready for a new take, adding a scene if needed. It only appears in Key Map and MIDI Map modes, for mapping.
        • Launching a scene doesn't record into its empty armed slots unless Start Recording on Scene Launch is on in the Record, Warp & Launch Settings.

      • 19.3.3 Overdub Recording MIDI Patterns

        • To build a drum pattern or melody layer by layer in a looping clip:
          • Set Global Quantization to one bar, and choose a Record Quantization value if notes should be quantized as they're recorded.
          • Double-click an empty Session slot on the instrument's MIDI track to make an empty one-bar clip; change its loop length if needed.
          • Arm the track and press Session Record. Notes are added on every loop pass.
          • Press Session Record to switch between overdubbing and playing along without recording.
        • Option/Alt-double-click an empty slot to create the clip, arm the track and launch it in one step.
        • Undo removes the last pass; notes can be drawn, moved or deleted in the MIDI Note Editor while recording continues.

      • 19.3.4 MIDI Step Recording

        • Step recording enters notes with the transport stopped, at your own pace.
        • Arm the track, turn on Preview in the clip's MIDI Editor, and click where recording should start.
        • Hold notes and press the right arrow to enter them and move the insert marker one grid step; keep holding and press it again to lengthen them. Hold the notes and press the left arrow to delete them.
        • The step arrows can be MIDI mapped.
